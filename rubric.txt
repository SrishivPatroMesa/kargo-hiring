KARGO HIRING RUBRIC - Product Manager (PM) and Senior Product Manager (SPM)
Derived from 8 past hire profiles, not from the job descriptions.

PATTERNS FOUND (Exceeds Expectations vs Meets / Below)

Pattern 1 - They did the operational work themselves before doing their current job.
  All five "Exceeds" hires spent real time doing freight / logistics work with their own hands:
  Rohan ran import-export documentation for 180+ shipments a month at a CHA firm at JNPT;
  Sunita handled B/Ls and customs paperwork for 200+ shipments a month; Aditya managed berth,
  detention and DO-release escalations at a JNPT terminal; Meghna sorted out a customs hold
  overnight with the CHA; Lavanya did carrier allocation and exception management for 800+ shipments.
  Vikram (HR-tech PM), Rahul (fintech marketing) and Preetham (e-commerce backend, touched 3PLs only
  through APIs) never did the operational work.

Pattern 2 - They noticed something broken, fixed it without being asked, and other people adopted the fix.
  Rohan built an Excel shipment tracker because "the team had no reliable way to know where each
  shipment stood" - 12-person team adopted it in two weeks; later prototyped a B/L verification tool
  over a weekend, 30 colleagues using it within a month. Sunita redesigned her team's intake workflow
  over a weekend when a vendor changed its export format; retained permanently. Meghna's onboarding
  checklist became the CS team standard. Lavanya's Excel visibility dashboard was adopted by 2 other
  regional teams. The Meets/Below hires describe things they shipped as part of their assigned job
  (Vikram's 12 features, Preetham's index tuning), not fixes they started on their own because
  something was broken in front of them.

Pattern 3 - They write down what failed and what changed because of it.
  Aditya spent 4 months on a deal that was lost, wrote the post-mortem (wrong economic buyer, misread
  procurement cycle) and it became standard practice. Lavanya killed 2 features when usage data
  contradicted discovery, and wrote the internal and customer-facing outage post-mortem. Meghna found an
  undocumented product limitation, filed it and ran customer comms through a 6-week fix. Rohan led a
  vendor migration after diagnosing reliability problems. Vikram's, Rahul's and Preetham's CVs contain
  only successes - no killed work, no loss, no post-mortem.

SCORING SCALE (used for every criterion)
  5 = matches "strong" clearly, with specifics (numbers, named outcome, named situation)
  4 = mostly strong, one element thin
  3 = partial - the behaviour appears once or vaguely
  2 = weak - only implied or only in generic wording
  1 = matches "weak" / no evidence in the CV
Criterion score -> weighted total out of 100 = sum(weight x score / 5).

=== PM ===

Criterion name: Hands-on operations time
What a strong candidate looks like: Has personally done ground-level operations work in freight, logistics, supply chain, ports, warehousing or a similar physical-operations business (handled shipments, documents, carriers, customs, dispatch, exceptions) for at least a year, and the CV names concrete volumes or situations (e.g. "managed 400+ movements monthly", "resolved customs holds"). Operations work in another physical domain (manufacturing floor, field ops, healthcare ops) scores 3-4.
What a weak candidate looks like: Has only worked on software for operations users from a desk - talks about "users", "stakeholders" or "integrations with 3PLs" but has never done the operational job; or no operations exposure at all.
Weight: 30%
Source: Rohan Desai (CHA documentation, JNPT), Sunita Krishnamurthy, Aditya Shetty (JNPT terminal), Meghna Tiwari, Lavanya Iyer (Mahindra Logistics). Absent in Vikram Nair, Rahul Bose, Preetham Rao.

Criterion name: Unprompted fix that others adopted
What a strong candidate looks like: At least one specific case where they spotted a broken or missing process that was not their assigned work, built a fix themselves quickly (days or weeks - a sheet, tool, checklist, prototype, process), and other people kept using it - the CV says who adopted it and how many (e.g. "adopted by the 12-member team within two weeks", "became the branch's standard").
What a weak candidate looks like: Only lists features or projects that were their assigned job, or improvements with no sign anyone else adopted them; generic lines like "drove process improvements" or "streamlined workflows" with no trigger, no artefact and no adopters.
Weight: 25%
Source: Rohan Desai (Excel tracker; weekend B/L prototype), Sunita Krishnamurthy (weekend workflow redesign), Meghna Tiwari (onboarding checklist), Lavanya Iyer (visibility dashboard).

Criterion name: Kills and post-mortems
What a strong candidate looks like: The CV names something that did not work - a feature they killed, a deal they lost, an outage, a failed launch, a wrong assumption - and states what they concluded and what changed afterwards (redirected capacity, new practice, rewritten process). Bonus if they wrote the post-mortem themselves.
What a weak candidate looks like: Every bullet is a success. No killed work, no loss, no reversal, no lesson. "Shipped N features" with no mention of what was dropped.
Weight: 20%
Source: Aditya Shetty (lost-deal post-mortem became standard), Lavanya Iyer (killed 2 features on data; outage post-mortem), Meghna Tiwari (product limitation through 6-week fix). Absent in Vikram Nair, Rahul Bose, Preetham Rao.

Criterion name: No layer between them and the consequence
What a strong candidate looks like: Worked directly with the people affected by their decisions and owned the outcome without a manager or intermediary layer in between - e.g. "sole PM", "no account manager layer between client and execution", "translated field requirements without a product layer", "no escalation to management in 14 months". For PM, one clear instance is enough for a 5.
What a weak candidate looks like: Always one of several people in a team with a senior person above making the call ("supported senior PMs", "part of a 12-engineer team", "presented roadmap to CTO for approval"); customer contact only through other teams.
Weight: 15%
Source: Lavanya Iyer ("sole PM"; "made calls we trusted immediately"), Aditya Shetty ("no account manager layer"), Rohan Desai ("without a product layer"), Meghna Tiwari. Contrast: Vikram Nair (one of a 4-PM team, roadmap to CTO), Preetham Rao (one of 12 engineers).

Criterion name: Proof by usage, not credentials
What a strong candidate looks like: Results are stated as what other people did differently - adoption, active use, fewer tickets, retention, on-time shipments - with numbers (e.g. "3x weekly active use within 30 days", "support tickets down 60%"). Certifications and titles are incidental.
What a weak candidate looks like: The CV leans on credentials, frameworks, talks, certifications, or output counts ("shipped 12 features", "Product School CPO", "speaker at...") with little evidence of how users' behaviour changed.
Weight: 10%
Source: Lavanya Iyer and Meghna Tiwari (usage/churn outcomes) vs Vikram Nair (certifications, speaking, feature counts).

=== SPM ===

Criterion name: Hands-on operations time
What a strong candidate looks like: Two or more years doing ground-level operations work in freight, logistics, supply chain or ports themselves (shipments, documentation, carriers, customs, exceptions), AND has since carried that knowledge into product or systems decisions - the CV links an operational problem they lived to something they later built or decided. Operations in another physical domain scores 3.
What a weak candidate looks like: Domain knowledge only through customers, research or integrations; or operations time that is short (a rotation or internship) and never connected to later product work.
Weight: 20%
Source: Rohan Desai (ops at JNPT -> built the B/L module he once did by hand), Lavanya Iyer (3PL carrier ops -> carrier integration product).

Criterion name: Unprompted fix that others adopted
What a strong candidate looks like: Multiple cases (at least two) of spotting a broken process outside their remit, building the fix themselves, and seeing it adopted beyond their own team - other teams, branches, or the whole company - with the adopter count stated. At least one should be a standard or system others now run on.
What a weak candidate looks like: One small team-internal improvement, or improvements described without a trigger, artefact or adopters; or only assigned roadmap work.
Weight: 20%
Source: Rohan Desai (tracker adopted by 12; prototype to 30 users, now a core feature), Lavanya Iyer (dashboard adopted by 2 other regional teams; first carrier review process at the branch).

Criterion name: Kills and post-mortems
What a strong candidate looks like: Names a significant call that went wrong or was reversed (killed product line, lost major deal, outage, failed migration), shows they owned the write-up, and states a lasting change that resulted (new standard, changed strategy, practice adopted by others).
What a weak candidate looks like: No failures or reversals at all; or failures mentioned only as someone else's problem they cleaned up, with no conclusion drawn.
Weight: 20%
Source: Aditya Shetty (post-mortem became team practice), Lavanya Iyer (kills + outage post-mortem with follow-ups to closure).

Criterion name: No layer between them and the consequence
What a strong candidate looks like: Sustained periods (a year or more, ideally more than once) as the final decision-maker for an area with no senior person above approving their calls - sole PM, founder or founding team, solo consultant, first hire in a function - including decisions with long-lived consequences (vendor/platform choices, migrations, what not to build), and they lived with the result.
What a weak candidate looks like: Senior titles but decisions routed through a Head/VP/committee; "aligned stakeholders" or "presented to leadership" as the main verbs; ownership only of execution, not of the call.
Weight: 30%
Source: Sunita Krishnamurthy (independent consultant, scoped and ran a full system migration), Rohan Desai (led vendor migration; most senior engineer in the room), Lavanya Iyer (sole PM). Contrast: Vikram Nair.

Criterion name: Proof by usage, not credentials
What a strong candidate looks like: Outcomes stated as changes in other people's behaviour or business results that trace to their decisions - adoption, retention, churn, integration time, unblocked deals - with numbers, across more than one role.
What a weak candidate looks like: Leans on credentials, frameworks, titles, team size or output counts; outcomes vague ("drove growth", "improved efficiency").
Weight: 10%
Source: Meghna Tiwari (6% churn vs 24% team), Lavanya Iyer (3x weekly active use). Contrast: Vikram Nair.
